What survives aWISH, and what a successor could do with it

For whoever picks this up in 2027. aWISH was an €8m EU project that put cameras and sensors into six slaughterhouses to score animal welfare on the line and feed the scores back to farms. It closed on 31 October 2026 with no plan for what comes next. This page lists what it left behind, which of its 19 technologies are most promising, what a programme around each would avert per dollar, and the two forms a successor could take. Detail sits under each section; the source and page for every claim are in the verification log published with packs A and B, and section 8 lists what is submitted with this page.

Bottom line. Only the broiler policy play (ChickenCheck footpad and hock burn) clears AIM's bar. The pig technologies are a watch until aWISH's pig results are public. Without a founder by mid-2027, the default is no successor.

1aWISH in one page

What it was, where it ran, what survives on its own and what does not. Condensed from the handover brief (available on request) and the project profile.

What it was. Animal Welfare Indicators at the Slaughterhouse (aWISH). A Horizon Europe project, €8.0m, 28 partners in 11 countries, November 2022 to 31 October 2026, coordinated by ILVO, the Flanders Research Institute for Agriculture, Fisheries and Food, in Belgium. Fattening pigs and broilers only. The objective was to evaluate and improve welfare

via automated monitoring of animal-based welfare indicators at the slaughterhouse in order to give feedback and advice on best practices

to the farmer, the catching team, the transporter and the slaughterhouse. It set out to produce five things:

  • an AWI Catalogue of animal welfare indicators (AWIs) that can be measured automatically at slaughter
  • a central data platform and feedback tool giving each actor per-batch feedback and benchmarking
  • integrated welfare assessment at farm, chain, regional and national level, plus an intervention study
  • nine Best Practice Guides
  • stakeholder engagement with an expert panel

Where. Six pilot slaughterhouses in six countries, each co-led by a research partner:

  • Vion Groenlo, Netherlands (pigs)
  • Batallé, Spain (pigs)
  • DUC Chailley, France (broilers)
  • Plukon Sieradz, Poland (broilers)
  • Grossfurtner, Austria (pigs)
  • Carnex, Serbia (pigs)

With what. Sensors from vendors and from research partners:

  • carcass-lesion and footpad cameras (CLK)
  • a pig stunning-effectiveness sensor (Nuna Solutions)
  • lung and liver lesion sensors at evisceration, and on-farm weight and climate sensors (Innotech Vision / PLF Agritech)
  • a stress-vocalisation monitor and a blood stress assay (FBN, the Research Institute for Farm Animal Biology, Germany)
  • broiler sound and image tools for the farm and transport (Itavi, the French poultry technical institute)
  • barn-climate sensors (Bit Gear)
  • a tear-staining camera (ILVO) and a tail camera (Universitat Autònoma de Barcelona, UAB, with Selección Batallé)

Why continue it? Four reasons:

  • epidemiological welfare data the movement lacks
  • correlating welfare outcomes with farming practices
  • a basis for policy and certification thresholds
  • the choke point: far fewer slaughterhouses than farms, so instrumenting plants reaches more animals per unit of effort

The caveats are under "No successor" in section 5.

2Three primers

For a reader who has never been inside a plant: the slaughterhouse, where the sensors sit, and what "validated" means in this project.

A. The slaughterhouse

Verified

The EU slaughters about 222 million pigs and about 6.3 billion broilers a year (2024; the pig figure is Eurostat's, the broiler figure an estimate from Eurostat's chicken count, which also includes spent hens). Most of them go through a few dozen industrial plants per major country, run by a handful of companies. Germany's top ten pig slaughterers handled three quarters of 44.9 million pigs in 2025; four companies dominate Dutch pig slaughter. Margins are thin: Vion's EBITDA (earnings before interest, tax, depreciation and amortisation) was 1.5 % of revenue in 2023, and Westfleisch's 2024 operating result spread over its pigs is about €2.9 per pig, the same order as what tail lesions and condemnations cost per pig (Irish data, 2010–12).

  • A fixed camera sees each pig carcass for 2 to 9 seconds and each broiler for a fifth of a second at 18,000 birds an hour. That is the engineering constraint behind every sensor below.
  • Plants say stunning almost never fails; auditors say it often does. CO₂ plants self-report 0 to 0.05 % ineffective stuns; field audits found signs of insufficient stun depth in 7.5 % of CO₂-stunned pigs. About two thirds of EU pigs are CO₂-stunned.
  • Camera mandates are arriving (Spain since 2023; Germany adopted one on 5 Nov 2026; Flanders in principle; a Dutch draft has been through public consultation but has not been found before parliament), but none yet asks for automated analysis. The Dutch draft is the only text that expressly contemplates prescribing it.
  • No single count of EU slaughterhouses exists. Eurostat publishes none, and national lists of approved plants run to hundreds while the volume sits in a few dozen.

B. Where the sensors sit

Verified

Each aWISH sensor sits at one point on the line and sees one thing. What it sees decides who can act on it: footpad dermatitis and hock burn clearly originate on the farm weeks earlier, so a slaughter score is only useful as feedback to the grower; a failed stun is seconds old and the plant can act on it directly; catch damage could come from catching, transport or lairage and is hard to assign.

  • Unloading and lairage (pigs): AI4Animals handling CCTV (Deloitte, at Vion); STREMODO microphones for stress calls (FBN), installed in the unloading and lairage area.
  • Stunner exit (pigs): BlinkAI eye camera (Nuna). The stunner exit is where practice diverges most from guidance: aWISH's own guide (deliverable D4.3) asks for sticking within 20 seconds of gondola exit, the commonly cited maximum stun-to-stick interval is 60 seconds, and the one recent field study we found (Sweden, 2023–24, five plants) measured a mean of 90 seconds, range 32–199.
  • Carcass line after singeing (pigs): PigInspector for skin, ear and tail lesions and tail length (CLK); the UAB tail camera; ILVO's tear-staining camera at Batallé (station not stated).
  • After plucking (broilers): ChickenCheck catch damage and scratch cameras on the carcass, and footpad and hock burn on the separated feet (CLK).
  • Evisceration (pigs): Lesion-Detect lung and liver lesions (Innotech), at Batallé and Carnex.
  • Not on the slaughter line: EBroilerTrack sound and image (Itavi) on farm and in transport; the farm and transport sensors in the ranking below.

C. What "validated" means here

Verified

The project's own bar (D2.2) is agreement with a trained human scorer: a correlation above 0.75, or accuracy, sensitivity and specificity stated and adequate. Against that bar, three aWISH-period results are peer-reviewed: ChickenCheck footpad (κ 0.70) and hock burn (κ 0.60), Weight-Detect on farm, and Lesion-Detect lungs. Everything else on the pig slaughter line is preliminary.

  • The Final Event slides (annual meeting of the European Federation of Animal Science, EAAP, Hamburg, 10 Sep 2026) add two warnings. CLK's tail-length method had a mean error of 1.63 cm at the Austrian plant it was trained on and "external validation has failed" at the Dutch plant (mean error 2.88 cm, limits of agreement about ±7 cm against a ±2 cm bar). BlinkAI could not read more than half of 3,765 pigs and flagged 68 positives where human checks found 30.
  • No controlled study we found links a camera score plus a consequence to a lesion reduction. The effect sizes used in section 4 are borrowed from national before-and-after data.
  • Authorities' stated barrier to acting on automated scores is lack of external validation (the Dutch food safety authority, NVWA). That is the opening for an independent validation body, and the reason the pig results in Q6 matter more than any other open question.

3The technology ranking

A weighted-factor model (WFM) over the 19 technologies aWISH tested, ranking the 18 still held by someone by how much a successor should care about each. Seven factors, 0–10. The base weights are derived from a meta-model rather than set by hand; two alternative weightings test robustness. Built with Claude. The 0–10 scores are judgements drafted from the handover packs and checked by a second AI pass.

Read it this way. ChickenCheck footpad and hock burn ranks first because it is the only row with a 10 on both action link (the mandate in Germany's QS scheme, Qualität und Sicherheit, and Dutch and French schemes) and prevalence, and the gap is policy reach, not the technology. PigInspector and the UAB tail camera are the two pig rows that stay in the top four under every weighting. The ranking is not fully robust: under tractability-heavy weights STREMODO comes first and ChickenCheck drops to eighth, because STREMODO has an institute holder and nobody else wants it.

What it leaves out on purpose. Species scale. The EU slaughters about 6.3 billion broilers a year against 222 million pigs (primer A), and the model uses species only as a sort key. The cost-effectiveness estimate in section 4 is where animal numbers enter, and they dominate it.

The base weights are each factor's meta-model total minus a cut line of 6.0, normalised to 100. The cut line is a choice: at 6.55 coverage nearly disappears and severity reaches 24. Encapsulation, how much of an animal's welfare the indicator stands for, is the one idea in Aaron Boddy's own metric model that this one leaves out.

Why the top rows rank where they do

ChickenCheck footpad and hock burn. On sale since 2011 with competitors, peer-reviewed, and both Plukon plants in the project (DUC in France and Sieradz in Poland) likely to keep it. We estimate that about a quarter of EU broilers (range 16–41 %) are camera-scored for footpad today, almost all in Germany and the Netherlands, and about 14 % (8–22 %) have a camera score with a consequence for the grower. Diffusion follows rules, not hardware: Denmark installed cameras and still scores by hand.

PigInspector. High severity, prevalence and coverage, and the pig system a founder is most likely to meet again because CLK sells it. In aWISH it was a prototype (TRL 5) with the algorithm still under development in September 2025. No peer-reviewed aWISH validation; tail length failed external validation at the second plant.

UAB tail camera. Turns on one question to UAB. The Data Management Plan (DMP) commits research-side code and datasets to publication with the methodology paper, but no code or dataset has been found, and the preliminary F-scores are 55 on seven classes and 76 on four. Published code and the 1,542 labelled images would lift tractability; a refusal would drop it.

BlinkAI. A 10 on severity and coverage, but a technology that will exist without a successor and that could not read half the pigs in its validation run. Policy, through stunning checks under Regulation 1099/2009, could make it matter.

STREMODO. The only pig slaughterhouse sensor with both pre-aWISH evidence (r 0.78–0.87 against six experts) and an institute holder, which makes it the pig technology most likely to be licensed to a nonprofit. But no scheme acts on its index, it has no payer, and AI4Animals' "audio analytics / stress calls" feature may already cover lairage sound at Vion.

4What the top three are worth per dollar

A back-of-the-envelope cost-effectiveness estimate for a $1M programme built around each of the top three technologies, in the unit used by Ambitious Impact (AIM), suffering-adjusted days (SADs) averted per US dollar. Built with Claude on top of the ranking and a SADs model. Every input for which AIM has given an estimate is set to AIM's value: the discount rate (3.5 % for suffering, 6.4 % for money), the tail-biting durations, the whole-life skin-wound rows for pigs and AIM's broiler conventions. AIM review pending. The central figures and the step table are in the short workbook submitted with this page; the intervals and every share of simulations come from the full model, which is not submitted and is available on request. The big figure is the central estimate (every input at its central value); the range under it is the 90 % interval, the 5th to 95th percentile of 20,000 simulations.

Trust the ratios more than the levels. ChickenCheck beats PigInspector in 97 % of paired simulations, by a median of about 36 times, and the UAB camera in essentially all of them (full model). The gap is animal numbers and cost per animal, not suffering per animal: a pig brought under a consequence gains about 2.8 times what a bird does, but there are 6.3 billion broilers against 222 million pigs a year, one camera line covers about 48 million birds against 1.5 million pigs, and the pig cameras have not yet passed a validation gate. The levels rest on probabilities with limited empirical anchoring.

The founder's money buys the consequence, not the camera. In all three chains the result is carried by how much a programme raises the probability that a law, a scheme rule or a contract clause attaches a consequence to the slaughter score. The camera is shipped; what is missing is a sanction that is actually applied, which about 80 % of EU broilers sit outside today.

Only the broiler programme is in the range funders act on. AIM's bar is 11 SADs per $ (Aaron Boddy, AIM, 4 Nov). The ChickenCheck programme clears it in 84 % of the simulations, PigInspector in 6 %, the UAB camera in none (full model). AIM moved toward disaggregated pain-hours in April 2026, so pain-hours by category sit beside the scaled figure in the full model.

The $10k figure is an artefact of a huge lever and a tiny, unfalsifiable probability gain; its real product is information (does the clause have a sponsor?). The $10M tier cannot absorb the money in policy and is mostly validation and data; at 15 it sits just above AIM's bar of 11 and clears it in 49 % of simulations. PigInspector's $100k tier (7.9) is the only pig figure that clears the bar in a meaningful share of simulations (21 %).

How it could be wrong, largest first
  • The counterfactual inputs have limited empirical anchoring. How likely each lever is to land anyway, how much the money raises that, and how many years it brings the date forward are calibrated mainly to each other and to precedent rather than to data.
  • The effect sizes are borrowed. No controlled study links camera scores plus a consequence to lesion reductions; the broiler figure rests on confounded national before-and-after data, the pig figure on the broiler one by analogy.
  • Attribution. Eurogroup for Animals, Compassion in World Farming (CIWF) and the Royal Society for the Prevention of Cruelty to Animals (RSPCA) already lobby the broiler revision. Whether a new organisation adds an increment, or should hand them the evidence, is not known.
  • Execution risk is folded into the probabilities, not modelled. Funging and leverage are ignored.
  • The pig figure rests on one input chain. 91 % of PigInspector's figure is skin wounds: AIM's whole-life skin-wound row (one staff estimate citing older Welfare Footprint modelling) times our own estimate of the share a slaughter-line consequence can reach (0.5).
  • AIM's tail-biting durations make tail docking look good. Under them, docking lowers tail-related suffering about three times, against EU law (Directive 2008/120/EC) and the advocacy position. The pig figures use AIM's durations and should not be read as a view on docking.
  • The pig gates are judgements about unpublished results (Q6). Footpad pain-hours are our own estimate (AIM has no footpad row; Welfare Footprint folds it into lameness); the pig tail and skin rows are AIM's.
  • Do not add the two pig figures. Both rely on the same consequence and would count the same suffering twice.

What would improve the estimate most: AIM's pain-hours for broiler contact dermatitis, the source of AIM's tail-biting durations and the discount rate behind its bar of 11; the final results of aWISH's six pilots (work package 3, WP3) in place of the pig gates once Q6 is answered; a second rater for the probability inputs.

5Two shapes, costed, and the case for none

We drafted five successor shapes at four funding tiers. Fed with the ranking and the arithmetic above, two survive as something to cost: a policy play as the lead, and a custodian as the cheap floor that carries the open-technology ask. The other three get a line each. All costs are estimates.

Lead: the policy play

Shape C

Use aWISH's evidence and network to get certifiers, retailers and authorities to write automated, animal-based slaughter-line indicators into standards and law. The two rows that justify it are ChickenCheck footpad and hock burn, and BlinkAI through stunning checks.

  • The window. The EU Livestock Strategy of 7 July 2026 (COM(2026) 576, Action 16) promises a targeted revision of the rules for laying hens and broilers by end-2026, with practical on-farm welfare indicators. The ask: harmonised footpad and hock scoring at slaughter for all flocks, validated automated systems accepted as a method, mandatory feedback to growers, graduated consequences. The strategy puts the pigs proposal in April–June 2027. The Commission's work programme for 2027 (COM(2026) 710, 4 Nov 2026) lists no animal-welfare initiative. The poultry proposal could come in December 2026, before that programme starts; the pigs proposal's date falls inside it. Scoping the timing and who carries each file needs more work.
  • $100k buys it. A half-time policy lead hosted by Eurogroup or another EU non-governmental organisation (NGO); $80–150k a year for one full-time equivalent (FTE). First year: a vendor-neutral indicator annex for one private standard (QS as template, Beter Leven or RSPCA Assured as target), feed the Dutch camera bill's criteria, brief the Commission's Directorate-General for Health and Food Safety (DG SANTE).
  • $1M adds a second person and one commissioned validation study, plus a certifier pilot writing automated scoring into one label with Plukon's two ChickenCheck plants as reference sites, and a retailer outcome-measure clause with one continental retailer.
  • $10k buys one well-timed intervention: a response to the Commission's pigs consultation and one evidence note from the published broiler figures into the broiler revision, plus a meeting each with QS, RSPCA Assured and Dierenbescherming. On the arithmetic in section 4 this tier scores highest, about 140 SADs per $, but that is an artefact of a huge lever and a tiny probability gain that's hard to falsify. Our view: $10k buys information (does the clause have a sponsor, will a certifier cite a standard?), not impact, and its real cost-effectiveness is whatever it does to the $100k decision.

Strongest objection. Eurogroup already runs this policy line (2021 position paper, 2023 white paper) and CIWF and RSPCA lobby the same file, so a new organisation may add little beyond handing them the evidence. The pig loop is already proceeding commercially into QS without independent validation, so the ask rests on broiler evidence alone until Q6's answer surfaces. Whether the consortium's exploitation plan already assigns policy follow-up to Eurogroup is Q3.

Floor: the custodian

Shape A

Keep aWISH's relationships, catalogue, training material and platform knowledge warm and documented for 12–24 months, so a founder arriving in 2027 restarts from a live network rather than from zero. It fits the hypothesis that a successor is a custodian of relationships more than of a codebase. It is also where the one "pick up" conclusion from section 3 lives: the custodian asks UAB and Batallé to publish the tail camera's code and labelled images, and if they do, finds a home for extending the tool.

  • $10k buys keeping this handover current (the data contacts and the per-pilot asks) with 100–150 volunteer hours a year: a quarterly check-in with ILVO's exploitation lead and the six pilot co-leads, one refresh of the handover material at 12 months, and mirroring the deliverables still to land (among them D1.3, D1.7, D6.4 and the final WP3 results).
  • $100k buys a 0.4–0.5 FTE custodian inside an existing entity (an engineering consultancy, an EU NGO, or an academic host), custody of the domain, catalogue and e-course alongside BioSense, a yearly visit per pilot, and a read-only platform snapshot if Q1 comes back positive. Estimate: $60–90k a year, 0.5 FTE plus hosting and travel.
  • Biggest dependency: a founder candidate by mid-2027. Without one, custody has been paid for nothing. The two precedents, the Welfare Quality Network after its EU funding (the Sixth Framework Programme) ended and the European Association for Precision Livestock Farming (EA-PLF) as custodian of the EU-PLF project's blueprint, both decayed into a frozen website.

The three shapes that get a line

  • Data and epidemiology service (Shape B). Pool per-batch welfare data from cooperating plants and publish anonymised statistics. $300–600k a year at the $1M tier. Likely to fail due to data access: no pilot data is committed to open deposit, the DMP says all data remains private, and Germany, the Netherlands and Denmark already run closed national loops. However, a potential partner company already analysing slaughterhouse data has been identified and contacted. It has offered potential hosting of a successor project with this shape, depending on particulars. Contact available upon request.
  • Open technology (Shape D). Rebuild or license slaughter-line sensors under an open licence. $600k–1.2M a year for one open sensor at one plant. The IP it would inherit is almost entirely blocked, and for broiler footpad it would re-create products already at TRL 9.
  • Validation and outcome-data standards body (Shape E). An independent body that validates commercial systems and publishes a welfare-outcome data standard that certifiers and authorities can cite. $10k buys a v0.1 standard on Zenodo from volunteer hours; real validation studies start at $1M. The demand is on record (NVWA finds most systems lack external validation; QS names no accuracy threshold), but no one has yet asked a certifier whether it would cite one, and it depends on Q6 above all. It is where the $1M policy play's validation study points.

No successor

A real option, and one that seems more promising if three conditions come back negative:

  • the pig results in Q6 do not support a validated pig indicator
  • no pilot keeps its kit (Q4)
  • the data cannot move (Q2)

Commercial vendors and national schemes already carry broilers, stunning and handling CCTV regardless. What would then be lost is narrow: the pig lesion work, lairage sound, tear staining, the network and the cross-chain feedback loop. The cost is $0, with the European Commission and BioSense carrying the public record. This is the default outcome.
